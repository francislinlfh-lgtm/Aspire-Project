# Priority reading list — ten papers, in order

Read with a pencil. For each: what to extract for Aspire. (verify) = bibliographic
details to confirm; do not cite from this list without checking the source itself.

**Priority insert (2026-07-30), read alongside #1:** Bradford, Brunsdon & Ferguson
(2023), "Cognitive mechanisms of perspective-taking across adulthood: An eye-tracking
study using the director task," *JEP: Learning, Memory, and Cognition*, 49(6),
959–973, doi:10.1037/xlm0001190 — **the source paper of the verified Study 1A
dataset (OSF 2epsu)**. Extract: exact design (Listener vs Shared conditions; what
Contrast/Distractor/AF/F codes mean), trial counts and exclusions, what ScalarTime
is locked to in the eye-tracking file, RT conventions (negative values), and their
analysis choices. You cannot fit data whose design you haven't read. Open-access
manuscript: kar.kent.ac.uk/96936.

1. **Heller, Parisien & Stevenson (2016), Cognition** — "Perspective-taking behavior
   as the probabilistic weighing of multiple domains."
   *The* paper. Extract: exact model equations, salience treatment, fitted parameter
   values, what data they fit and how, and — critically — whether they already did
   formal serial-vs-simultaneous model comparison. Our P-MIX label ([ADAPT] → [LIT])
   and half the research gap depend on this reading.

2. **Keysar, Barr, Balin & Brauner (2000), Psychological Science** — "Taking
   perspective in conversation: The role of mutual knowledge in comprehension."
   The paradigm. Extract: exact stimuli (note: vague "small," not superlatives),
   display structure, error rates and fixation measures, and their own statement of
   the perspective-adjustment account.

3. **Barr (2008), Cognition** — "Pragmatic expectations and linguistic evidence:
   Listeners anticipate but do not integrate common ground."
   Time-course methodology and the anticipation/integration distinction — the
   dependent-variable playbook for D1.

4. **Hanna, Tanenhaus & Trueswell (2003), JML** — early but partial common-ground
   integration. Extract: the graded-constraint reading and why P-CG is a strawman.

5. **Epley, Keysar, Van Boven & Gilovich (2004), JPSP** — "Perspective taking as
   egocentric anchoring and adjustment."
   The process claims our P-ANCHOR failed to capture: effort, time pressure,
   adjustment dynamics. Foundation for any faithful serial formalization in D1.

6. **Epley & Gilovich (2006), Psychological Science** — why adjustments are
   insufficient. Basis of the provisional choice-level discriminator
   (IDENTIFIABILITY.md §3.6).

7. **Rubio-Fernández (2017), Psychonomic Bulletin & Review** — "The director task: A
   test of Theory-of-Mind use or selective attention?"
   The construct-validity critique of our own paradigm. Read as opposition prep; it
   also motivates implementing an attention-based account as a future policy.

8. **Frank & Goodman (2012), Science** — "Predicting pragmatic reasoning in language
   games." The quantitative-fitting standard the project aspires to (RSA); also the
   likely reviewer lens on anything we submit.

9. **Roberts & Pashler (2000), Psychological Review** — "How persuasive is a good
   fit?" The epistemology of model fitting; the reason our protocol is
   fit-then-predict and our mimicry caveats exist. Pair with Pitt & Myung (2002,
   TICS), "When a good fit can be bad."

10. **Visuospatial Perspective Taking in Multimodal Language Models (2026),
    arXiv:2603.23510** — the existing LLM director-task evaluation. Extract: their
    stimuli, prompting, DVs, and exact claims — Study 1C must be differentiated
    against this paper line-by-line.

Second tier (verify existence/venues before relying on them): Brown-Schmidt & Heller
review chapter on perspective-taking in conversation (verify venue); Lin, Keysar &
Epley (2010, JESP) on cognitive load; Mozuraitis, Stevenson & Heller on production-side
mixture modeling (verify); Grodner & Sedivy (2011) on speaker reliability (verify);
Ryskin / Brown-Schmidt adaptation and dataset papers (verify OSF holdings — this
doubles as the Study 1A data audit); Kennedy (2007, Linguistics & Philosophy) on
vague adjectives, needed the moment stimuli move from superlatives to "small."
